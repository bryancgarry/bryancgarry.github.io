Procedural Planet Viewer

A procedurally generated planet with oceans, clouds, a ray-marched atmosphere, an orbiting moon, and a spacecraft you can fly between them.

ROLE
Solo developer
CONTEXT
JHU Applied Computer Graphics, final project (Spring 2026)
TECH
C++, OpenGL, GLSL, SDL3
SOURCE
GitHub (coming soon)

OVERVIEW

Inspired by games like Outer Wilds and Kerbal Space Program, the goal was a simple planet built up in layers of detail until it reads as a believable celestial body. The terrain is generated on the GPU, then wrapped in separate ocean, cloud, and atmosphere layers. A moon orbits the planet, and a spacecraft can be flown around both under their gravity.

TECHNICAL HIGHLIGHTS

  • Procedural terrain: a subdivided icosphere displaced entirely in the vertex shader using noise.
  • Ocean: a separate icosphere at sea level with perturbed normals for specular sparkle, plus Fresnel reflectance and depth-based coloring.
  • Clouds: fBm noise sampled on a shell around the planet and offset over time by a speed and drift direction to suggest wind.
  • Atmosphere: ray marching in a fragment shader, carrying ideas from the ray tracing assignment over to real-time rendering.
  • Moon: reuses the planet shader without terrain coloring for a natural grey surface, and orbits the planet.
  • Spacecraft: each body has a gravitational sphere of influence that pulls on the ship. It can switch between first and third person, and can even get carried along by the orbiting moon.

CHALLENGES & SOLUTIONS

Because the terrain only exists in the vertex shader, the CPU has no direct knowledge of the surface height. I tried sampling the shader's terrain data back into world space for collision, but couldn't get it fully correct in time, so the final version uses spherical collision against each body.

The atmosphere was the hardest part. Even with the ray marching working, tuning the scattering coefficients to get a convincing glow took a lot of iteration.

WHAT'S NEXT

Generating a whole set of planets with different colors and properties to push the shaders further, and adding asteroids or projectiles from the spacecraft to better show off collision and gravity.
