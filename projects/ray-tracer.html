Ray Tracer

A recursive ray tracer built on top of a scene graph framework, rendering a marble room full of reflective and refractive gems.

ROLE
Solo developer
CONTEXT
JHU Applied Computer Graphics, Project 1 (Spring 2026)
TECH
C++
SOURCE
GitHub (coming soon)

OVERVIEW

Rather than starting from scratch, I extended my scene graph framework from the previous course with ray intersection and shading. The scene is a room with a checkerboard floor, colored marble walls, a large ellipsoid mirror on the back wall, two image-textured pillars, a pair of diamonds and a torus, and three gem spheres modeled on the reflective and refractive properties of ruby, sapphire, and emerald.

TECHNICAL HIGHLIGHTS

  • Recursive tracing: reflection and refraction rays are traced up to a maximum depth, with adaptive termination once a ray's contribution falls below a threshold.
  • Scene graph traversal: rays walk the same scene graph used for rasterization, carrying matrix, material, and texture state down the tree.
  • Analytic intersection: spheres, quads, ellipsoids, and finite cylinders, with rays transformed into each object's local space through its inverse modeling matrix.
  • Triangle meshes: Möller–Trumbore ray/triangle intersection, tested against a local bounding box first.
  • Bounding hierarchy: AABB nodes group objects so rays that miss a group skip all of its children.
  • Lighting: Phong shading with shadow rays, refraction via Snell's law, and two colored lights for tinted shadows and highlights.
  • Textures: UV image texturing on the pillars, plus procedural checkerboard and Perlin noise marble textures.

CHALLENGES & SOLUTIONS

Triangle meshes showed speckled shading artifacts ("acne"). Offsetting reflection and refraction rays by a small epsilon reduced self-intersection, and I narrowed the remaining issue down to the triangle intersection math.

WHAT'S NEXT

Fully resolving the triangle mesh artifacts, and building the bounding hierarchy automatically instead of by hand.
